A commercial imaging sat just grabbed a 61.8-km closeup of Hubble — and the detail is wild

Vantor's WorldView Legion 4 snapped a shot of Hubble from just 61.8 km away on April 23, 2025 — close enough that you can see the open aperture door and the texture on the thermal blankets. The frame was captured during a routine slew that put the two satellites within 70 km of each other for roughly nine seconds.

What makes this notable isn't the proximity (which has happened before, just not from a commercial Earth-observation platform) but the resolution and pointing accuracy — Legion 4 hit Hubble with a 0.30 m ground-sample-distance camera that was designed to read license plates from orbit, not photograph other spacecraft.

Why the imagery matters

For one, it confirms that commercial smallsat constellations now have the optical chops to do close-range inspection of high-value assets. NASA has been quietly evaluating whether that capability could be repurposed for in-situ servicing reconnaissance — looking for thruster anomalies on aging assets like Hubble before a Crew Dragon goes up to swap a gyroscope.

"You can see the alignment of the open aperture door against the spacecraft body — that single frame tells you more about the structural state of HST than the last six months of telemetry." — Maxar engineer, on background.

The second-order story is policy. Vantor didn't ask NASA before pointing a 0.30m camera at a federally-owned spacecraft. There is no rule against it. There may be one soon.
